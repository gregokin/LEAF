# Precedents for spatial individual-plant demography coupled to multilayer soil water, and generic CPU/GPU consequences

Scope: public papers, public code and official docs only. “Verified” = read directly from the cited source (code file, open-access text, publisher/Crossref/OpenAlex metadata). “Inference” = my reading or reasoning. Publisher full texts for Tietjen 2009/2010, Peters 2002, Caylor 2004a/b/2005 and Palmquist 2018 were blocked (HTTP 403) or not text-extractable, so for those I rely on abstracts, metadata, open descendants, and code.

## 1. Tietjen et al. 2009 (WRR) and Tietjen et al. 2010 (Ecohydrology)

**Verified.** The 2009 Crossref abstract describes “a generic, process-based, spatially explicit soil moisture model of two soil layers”, tested at Israeli sites, and stresses intra-annual (sub-annual) precipitation variability. The 2010 OpenAlex abstract states the model simulates daily soil moisture in two layers with vegetation as two functional types, shrubs and grasses, that compete for water, applied to a Namibian thornbush savanna, including CO2 effects on water-use efficiency. The lineage (EcoHyD) is described in the open-access Irob et al. 2022 paper: “a total area of 2.25 ha consisting of 30 × 30 grid cells, with a 5 × 5 m² resolution”; “Annual grasses only have access to water in the upper soil layer, while perennial grasses and shrubs have access to water in both layers according to their root distribution”; vegetation is cover per cell; “The fate of each strategy type is calculated with a biweekly resolution during the wet season”; water is daily; grass dispersal is spatially uniform within 150 m², shrub dispersal uses a local kernel; grazing/browsing remove biomass; R scripts/output are on figshare.

**Inference / caveats.** Cell size and the three meta-PFTs are verified only for the 2022 version; I could not confirm the 2009/2010 grid size. Vegetation is a **cover fraction per cell, not individuals**, so there are no plant positions, no roots crossing cells, and no allometry. Reusable: the two-layer water budget structure (infiltration/evaporation/transpiration per layer split by PFT root fractions), the “growth linked to transpiration” WUE-style accounting, and the daily-water / biweekly-vegetation / annual-mortality time-scale separation. Missing for an individual-based design: individual demography, size structure, root geometry; terrain burial/erosion not found in inspected sources.

## 2. STEPWAT2 (revision 003cb9b4) with SOILWAT2 (submodule b05f410)

All statements below are from the pinned revision unless noted; the current master may differ.

**Individuals and state (verified, ST_structs.h, ST_indivs.c).** `indiv_st` holds `id`, `age`, `relsize` (“Size relative to a full-sized individual”), `res_required`, `res_avail`, `pr`, and `Next/Prev` pointers: individuals are a doubly-linked list per species (`Species[sp]->IndvHead`), allocated with `Mem_Calloc` and freed on death. **No x/y position, canopy radius, or per-individual root geometry exists.** “Individual-based” here means discrete plants with size and age, competing within a 1 m² plot: `plot.in` sets `plotsize` = 1.0 m² (“Everything revolves around the plotsize”), and densities/seed inputs in #/m² are converted to #/plot. Biomass = `relsize × mature_biomass` (grams).

**Water-to-resource coupling (verified, sxw.c, sxw_resource.c, ST_resgroups.c).** Each STEPWAT year, `SXW_Run_SOILWAT` converts resource-group biomass to SOILWAT2 vegetation inputs (biomass, % live, litter, cover) and runs a full daily year of SOILWAT2; monthly transpiration by layer is collected into `transpTotal`/`transpVeg[k]` and smoothed with a multi-year “transpiration window”. Allocation to functional groups is `use_by_group[g] += _roots_active_rel[g,l,p] * transp[l,p]` over groups × layers × months, where `_rootsXphen` = root profile × phenology and the relative root activity is rescaled annually by biomass. Within a group, `res_required = relsize × mature_biomass × live_biomass`, so individuals receive water proportional to size; `pr = res_required/res_avail` reduces the growth modifier (`gmod /= pr` when pr > 1), and growth is logistic: `growth1 = gmod × intrin_rate × (1 − relsize)`. Surplus water becomes “extra growth” removed at year end. **Inference:** this is a biomass-ratio partition of transpiration, not a WUE/carbon budget; biomass is the state and water is a limiting ratio.

**Time scales and water persistence (verified).** Plants are annual; SOILWAT2 is daily. In non-gridded mode soil water carries over between years. In gridded mode each cell is a complete plot whose globals (`Species`, `RGroup`, `Env`, `SXW`, soils) are swapped by `load_cell/unload_cell`; on each load the code re-initialises SOILWAT2 weather/flow/soil-temperature/soil-water with the comment “WARNING: Ideally, a grid cell should continue with the state that it ended the previous year” and “FIXME: remove once SOILWAT2 is re-entrant.” **So at this revision gridded soil water is not persistent across years**, a known limitation tied to SOILWAT2’s global state. Cells interact only by seed dispersal and colonization; `ST_seedDispersal.c` loops over all source–recipient cell pairs (O(N²)) with an exponential distance kernel and uniform draws; `MAX_CELLS` = 10000.

**Recruitment (verified).** Annuals establish from a seedbank with a beta-distributed establishment probability capped by `max_seed_estab`; perennials via `Species_NumEstablish` from fecundity parameters; clonal `max_vegunits` allows regrowth of killed individuals rather than adding new ones. RNG: `set_all_rngs` creates seven streams “reproducible and unique among RNGs, iterations, years and grid cells.” Types: `RealF` = float, `RealD` = double, tolerance macros `EQ/GT/LT` (generic.h).

**SOILWAT2 b05f410 (verified).** README: “an ecosystem water balance simulation model”; `MAX_LAYERS` 25, `MAX_TRANSP_REGIONS` 4, `NVEGTYPES` reported as 6 by my fetch (treat as uncertain), daily step, double precision, `transpiration[k][i]` per vegetation type k and layer i. The flow summary I obtained says potential transpiration is computed per type, then `remove_from_soil` is called per type in fixed order (“remove transp from swc”); **order dependence among types is my inference from that reading and should be checked in `SW_Flow.c`.**

**Reusable / adaptations.** Directly reusable ideas: root × phenology × layer-transpiration weighting; multi-year transpiration smoothing; per-cell RNG stream design; plot-area scaling of densities. Adaptations needed for a spatial individual model: replace linked lists with arrays, give individuals positions and footprints, make the water model re-entrant per cell (the FIXME above), and replace one-column-per-plot with roots over several cells. Missing: canopy overlap, lateral water flow, terrain/erosion, carbon/WUE.

**Palmquist et al. 2018 (abstract verified via USGS/CoMSES; full text 403).** “utilizes a process-based soil water model (SOILWAT2) to simulate available soil water in multiple soil layers, which plant individuals compete for based on the temporal matching of water and active root distributions with depth.” CoMSES lists code at Zenodo 1306924.

## 3. ECOTONE (Peters 2002)

**Verified (USDA ARS record).** An “individual-plant based simulation model” (ECOTONE) tracking recruitment, growth and mortality of individuals; coexistence of grasses and shrubs was driven by temporal and spatial partitioning of soil water across soil types. **Search-snippet level, not directly inspected:** a Jornada handbook / DTIC report describe ECOTONE as a gap model on a small plot (1–10 m²), annual plant step, with SOILWAT (Parton 1978) giving daily multilayer water, user-defined soil layers, and seed dispersal among plots. One automated pass over the Jornada PDF reported an 11 m × 11 m plot and no explicit x,y positions; a second pass could not extract text, so **plot size, layer count, the per-layer water allocation rule and position handling remain unverified.** Inference: ECOTONE is the closest conceptual ancestor to STEPWAT2 (gap plot + daily SOILWAT + species-specific root distributions + explicit recruitment stages), but no public source code was located.

## 4. Caylor et al. 2004 (GCB), Caylor & Shugart 2004 (LE), Caylor et al. 2005 (Ecosystems), Caylor et al. 2006 (WRR)

**Verified from abstracts/metadata.** GCB 2004: principal components of patch structure (leaf area, woody biomass, vertical leaf profiles) from field data parameterize an ecophysiology productivity model across four sites (879–365 mm). LE 2004: a daily terrestrial productivity model applied to horizontal distributions of structural parameters derived from 3-D canopy structure measured in the field at three sites. Ecosystems 2005: daily soil-moisture bucket with stochastic storm arrivals, Priestley–Taylor ET scaled by moisture, and five land-surface components (tree canopy; grass and bare soil under and between canopies); finding: “Tree canopies serve to reduce soil moisture stress of under-canopy vegetation in the middle of the rainfall gradient.” WRR 2006 (Crossref abstract): landscape water balance derived from “the statistics of an underlying poisson distribution of individual tree canopies” and an optimality argument (maximize uptake, minimize stress; roots shallower with more rain).

**Classification (inference from the above).** All four are **fixed-structure models**: measured or statistically generated tree/canopy structure drives productivity or water stress; none of the abstracts describes recruitment, growth or mortality loops. They are a family of analyses by one group sharing field structure data and a daily bucket, but **whether they are one continuous code implementation is not publicly documented** (Caylor’s 2003 dissertation is access-restricted). Reusable: canopy-position-dependent partitioning of ET into under/between-canopy components, Poisson-canopy statistics for initial conditions and tests. Missing: demography, multilayer roots, lateral root overlap as explicit geometry.

## 5. Additional precedents (2–3 strongest)

**TROLL 4.0 (Maréchaux et al. 2025, GMD; verified from open text).** Trees are explicit 3-D individuals in 1 m³ voxels; belowground is layered with user-defined thickness, horizontally coarse (“e.g. 25 m × 25 m, as commonly implemented in gap models”). Root depth `RD = 0.35 dbh^0.54`, exponential fine-root profile, uptake weighted by root biomass and soil-to-root conductance per layer; water balance daily, transpiration half-hourly; wood NPP converted to Δdbh via stem volume and allometry; seed bank per 1 m² cell, recruitment into empty cells under light/water thresholds; mortality by hydraulic stress, carbon starvation and treefall. Critically: “Tree roots are distributed across vertical layers but do not spread across belowground voxels horizontally.” C++, wrapped by `rcontroll` (GPL-3; its README says it wraps 3.1.7, so version alignment is uncertain). *Why closer than a generic DGVM:* explicit positions, carbon-based allometric growth, conductance-weighted uptake from shared layered soil, and full demography with open code. *Missing:* roots crossing horizontal cells, a grass/PFT layer, terrain.

**Holdo 2013 (PLoS ONE; verified, open).** Horizontally implicit, eight layers to 70 cm; rooting strategies as Beta-shaped profiles; uptake per layer “depend[ing] on the moisture saturation of the soil layer and the root mass allocated to it” (Michaelis–Menten); daily; biomass gain distributed to keep the root shape; full code in Source S1. *Why relevant:* a compact, conservative per-layer, root-mass-weighted competition rule for multiple strategies sharing one column—transferable as the per-(cell,layer) kernel. *Missing:* positions, individuals.

**SEIB-DGVM (Sato et al. 2007; partially verified).** Search-level sources describe a 30 × 30 m explicit plot with individual trees competing for light, 20 soil layers of 0.1 m, uptake from the top `RootDepth` layers to wilting point; I could not open the paper or model-description document, so treat details as unverified. TreeGrass (Simioni et al. 2000/2003) is a further lead: 3-D, daily, radiation/water/production for tree–grass systems at Lamto, but root/soil discretization and demography were not verifiable here.

**Cross-cutting (inference).** WUE/carbon accounting appears in EcoHyD (growth ∝ transpiration), TROLL (NPP → allometry) and Holdo (assimilation → biomass); STEPWAT2/ECOTONE use biomass-ratio water limitation. PFT/species distinctions: SOILWAT2 veg types vs STEPWAT2 resource groups/species; EcoHyD meta-PFTs with strategy subtypes; TROLL species traits. Vegetative spread exists in STEPWAT2 (clonal regrowth) and EcoHyD (grass dispersal); none of the inspected sources implements terrain burial or erosion.

## 6. Generic array/GPU design consequences

Symbols: N cells, L layers, P plants, E nonzero plant–(cell,layer) overlap entries; a 32-bit float/index is 4 B, double 8 B.

**Structure-of-arrays vs linked records.** STEPWAT2’s per-individual `calloc` + doubly linked lists are hostile to coalesced access; NVIDIA’s best-practices guide makes unit-stride warp access the high-priority rule and shows strided/misaligned penalties. A SoA layout (one array per attribute, P entries, with a stable ID array and a free-list or periodic stream compaction) is the standard replacement. Dead plants are handled by a mask plus compaction between years so kernels iterate live plants densely.

**Sparse incidence and competing uptake.** Root footprints over cells and layers form a sparse P × (N·L) incidence matrix with E entries; CSR/COO/ELL/HYB trade-offs follow Bell & Garland (2009). Store at least two views: plant-major CSR (row ptr P+1, col index E, weight E) and the transposed cell-layer-major CSR. Bytes ≈ E × (4 idx + 4 weight) per view plus (P+1)·4 and (N·L+1)·4 row pointers; with 64-bit indices double the index terms. A conservative, race-free uptake needs three passes: (1) per-plant demand split by weights (plant-major gather/scatter); (2) per-(cell,layer) total demand via segmented reduction over the transposed CSR (no atomics, deterministic order) and scaling factor `min(1, supply/demand)`; (3) per-plant uptake = Σ_e demand_e × scale_e (gather). Water removed per (cell,layer) then equals exactly the sum granted, which is the conservation property SOILWAT2 achieves sequentially per vegetation type. If atomics are used instead, CUDA defines them as read-modify-write “guaranteed without interference from other threads”, and `atomicAdd` supports float/double, but floating-point accumulation order is not fixed, so results are not bit-reproducible run to run; sorted CSR + segmented reduction (or fixed-order per-cell loops) is the deterministic alternative.

**Irregular mapping and graph construction.** When plants recruit, grow or die, E changes. Rebuild with a counting pass (entries per plant from footprint radius and cell size), exclusive scan for row pointers, and a fill pass; the transposed view via a key sort by (cell,layer) or a second count/scan. This is annual-scale work (STEPWAT2 updates root tables annually) and can live on the CPU if P is modest, with only the arrays uploaded.

**Persistent vs scratch.** Persistent: soil water N × L (double, 8 B, as SOILWAT2 uses double), plant SoA state, incidence arrays, RNG counters. Phase-reusable scratch: per-(cell,layer) demand/scale (N·L), per-plant demand/uptake (P), per-entry temporaries (E). Preallocate once per year; avoid per-step allocation.

**Widths.** 32-bit indices suffice while P, N·L and E < 2^31; E can exceed this before P does, so size the E index type separately. Float is adequate for weights/relsize; keep the water balance in double to avoid drift in long runs, and use tolerance comparisons as SOILWAT2 does.

**Stable IDs, RNG, restart.** Counter-based generators (Philox/Random123) are stateless functions of (key, counter), so a draw keyed by (plant ID, year, stream) is independent of thread scheduling and compaction order and trivially restartable; STEPWAT2’s seven per-cell/year/iteration streams are the CPU analogue. Checkpoint = persistent arrays + counters.

**CPU ecology between GPU phases.** Daily water on N·L is the dominant repeated cost; annual demography (establishment, mortality, dispersal) is sparse and branchy and can remain CPU code if transfers are bounded (P × bytes per attribute per year), keeping soil state device-resident. STEPWAT2’s O(N²) pairwise dispersal would need a kernel-limited neighbourhood or a sorted-cell approach before scaling N.

No benchmarks or speedups are claimed; these are layout and determinism consequences derived from the cited primary docs.
